How this works
What is this page?

Each row is a model estimate snapshot for one upcoming dividend cycle. When the cycle completes the actuals are filled in automatically — so you can see how accurately the model predicted the dip timing, price, and entry zone.

How accuracy is measured

Predictions are logged before outcomes are known — this is walk-forward validation, not backtesting. Actuals are matched in automatically once the cycle resolves. There are three accuracy signals: Zone Hit Rate (did price enter the projected entry zone?), Date Error (how many days off was the timing?), and Price Error (how far off was the dip price?). Price error is best read for systematic bias — a consistently negative average means the model regularly underestimates how deep the dip will go.

Reading the numbers
Colour thresholds used in the KPI strip and table:
  • Zone hit rate — ≥ 70% good · ≥ 50% marginal · below 50% poor
  • Date error — ≤ 7 days good · ≤ 21 days acceptable · > 21 days poor
  • Price error — no pass/fail threshold; watch for consistent negative bias across resolved rows
  • Timing cards — compare like-for-like: Reliable vs Bimodal vs Unreliable. Bimodal date errors are naturally smaller because they are measured from the nearer cluster center
Comparing accuracy across timing types

Bimodal date errors will always look smaller than Reliable ones — not because Bimodal is more accurate, but because the error is measured from whichever of the two cluster centers happened to be closer to the actual. You always get the benefit of hindsight in choosing the closer target. This is a structural advantage built into the measurement, not a signal of better timing.

Because of this, never compare Reliable vs Bimodal date errors directly. Use the per-type timing cards above the table to evaluate each type on its own terms. For Bimodal stocks, the cluster hit tally (C1 vs C2 wins) is the more meaningful signal — it shows which timing window is genuinely predictive over time, independent of how small the measured error is.

Projected Ex-Div Date

The model's forward estimate of the next ex-dividend date for the series, built from historical dividend intervals. Everything downstream — the estimated low date, entry zone, resolution trigger, and ex-div error — is anchored to this date. For the projection formula, see In Depth section 1.

Estimated Low Date(s)
When the model expects the stock to dip before ex-div.
  • Reliable — one date window (median ± p25/p75 bounds from historical timing)
  • Bimodal — two windows: C1 early (farther from ex-div) and C2 late (closer to ex-div)
  • Unreliable — no date estimate; price zone is the only guide
Date Error

How many days off the dip-date estimate was. Negative = actual came earlier than forecast; positive = came later. For Bimodal stocks this is measured from whichever cluster center was closer to the actual — so Bimodal errors naturally look smaller. Use the timing cards above to compare like-for-like across types.

Cluster (C1 / C2)

Bimodal only. Records which cluster the actual low date fell closest to — C1 (early window, farther from ex-div) or C2 (late window, closer to ex-div). Either window can win for any given cycle. The timing cards show the running C1/C2 win tally — how often each window correctly called the dip across all resolved cycles. For historical avg dip depth per cluster, see the Cluster dominance row in the dashboard's Forward Setup section.

Ex-Div Error

How many days off the projected ex-div date was once the actual is known. Negative = ex-div happened earlier than forecast.

Price Error

How far off the estimated dip price was. Negative = stock dipped deeper than forecast.

Zone Outcome

Whether price traded inside the projected entry zone (Zone Hit), stayed too shallow and never entered it (Miss Above), or fell straight through the bottom of it (Miss Below).

First / Last Seen

When this specific estimate was active. If the model's date shifted by more than 7 days between pipeline runs, a new row is inserted rather than overwriting — so you can see how the estimate evolved toward the actual over time.

Resolved

An estimate becomes resolved once the projected ex-div date has passed. At that point the pre-ex-div window has closed, the cycle low is finalised, and actuals are filled in automatically.

0. Walk-forward validation

Estimates are committed as snapshots at first_seen, before the dividend cycle resolves. Actuals are matched in post-cycle from the stock's completed cycle history by ex-div date proximity. The model never uses future data to construct an estimate, and no parameters are fitted to the outcomes being measured here — making these out-of-sample forward-test results, not backtest figures.

1. Projected Ex-Div Date

Built from same-series clean dividend intervals with day-of-month drift detection. If the projected date lands in the past, the model steps forward by one interval until it is in the future.

2. Estimated Low Date — RELIABLE
est_low_date      = proj_exdiv_date − round(median_wks × 7)
est_low_date_from = proj_exdiv_date − round(p75_wks × 7)   ← earlier bound
est_low_date_to   = proj_exdiv_date − round(p25_wks × 7)   ← later bound

p25/p75 are interpolated from the sorted clean wks_before values (timing.vals).

3. Estimated Low Date — BIMODAL

Clean wks_before values are split at the largest gap. Lower values → Cluster 2 (late dip, fewer weeks before ex-div); higher values → Cluster 1 (early dip, more weeks before).

est_low_date_cluster1 = proj_exdiv_date − round(early_cluster_median × 7)
est_low_date_cluster2 = proj_exdiv_date − round(late_cluster_median  × 7)

Ranges = median ± 1 SD (population SD). When SD > 2 weeks, the range is replaced by a ±Nwk badge — the date shown is still the best estimate, but the timing spread is too wide to show a meaningful window. Single-point clusters (only one cycle in that cluster, SD = 0) show just the date with no badge.

4. Date Error
RELIABLE: date_error_days = actual_low_date − est_low_date
BIMODAL:  date_error_days = actual_low_date − nearest_cluster_center

Signed: negative = actual came earlier.

Nearest-cluster bias. For BIMODAL, nearest_cluster_center is always chosen in hindsight — whichever of C1/C2 had the smaller absolute gap to the actual. This guarantees BIMODAL errors are at most as large as the error to the worse cluster, and often considerably smaller. It is a structural measurement effect, not a sign that BIMODAL timing is inherently more precise. Cross-type date error comparisons are therefore meaningless — use the per-type timing cards and the C1/C2 win tally instead.

5. Cluster Hit (BIMODAL)
d1 = actual_low_date − est_low_date_cluster1
d2 = actual_low_date − est_low_date_cluster2
cluster_hit = 1  if |d1| ≤ |d2|,  else  2
6. Ex-Div Date Error
exdiv_date_error_days = actual_exdiv_date − proj_exdiv_date

Signed. Negative = ex-div occurred earlier than projected.

7. Price Error
price_error_pct = (actual_low_px − est_low_px) / est_low_px × 100

est_low_px = zone_anchor_price × (1 + median_dip / 100). Negative = dipped deeper than estimated.

8. Zone Outcome
ZONE_HIT        : zone_bot ≤ actual_low_px ≤ zone_top
ZONE_MISS_ABOVE : actual_low_px > zone_top  (dip shallower than zone)
ZONE_MISS_BELOW : actual_low_px < zone_bot  (dip overshot zone)
9. Estimate Change Tracking (First / Last Seen)

Each pipeline run builds a candidate row. If an existing row matches the same (ticker, series_id, proj_exdiv_date ±30d) and the estimated low dates are within 7 days, only last_seen is updated. A shift greater than 7 days inserts a new row, preserving the full history of how estimates evolved.

10. Resolution Trigger

Actuals are filled when a completed cycle in the stock's cycles array has exdiv_date within ±14 days of proj_exdiv_date, and proj_exdiv_date is in the past. At that point the pre-ex-div window has closed and the cycle low is final.

11. KPI strip & timing cards
zone_hit_rate  = zone_hit_count / total_resolved_count × 100   (%)
med_date_error = median( |date_error_days| )   across resolved rows that have a date estimate
Colour bands — zone hit rate : ≥70% green · ≥50% amber · <50% red
              date error     : ≤7d  green · ≤21d  amber · >21d  red

Timing cards repeat these figures per timing rating so RELIABLE and BIMODAL performance is not conflated. For BIMODAL cards the C1 vs C2 win tally records how often each timing window matched the actual dip across all resolved cycles — a long-run forward-test of cluster behaviour, independent of the cluster dominance computed at analysis time.
