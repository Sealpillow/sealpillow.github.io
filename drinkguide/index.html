Know your pour

A can of beer, a glass of wine and a shot of whisky hold about the same amount of alcohol. A bottle of soju holds five times that. This guide shows how strong each drink really is, and how much is sensible for you.

01

How strength is worked out

Strength depends on two things: how big the drink is and how much of it is alcohol. Multiply them together and you get the amount of pure alcohol, which is what your body actually has to deal with.

Volume (ml)× ABV %× 0.789÷ 10 g= standard drinks

ABV

Alcohol by volume, printed on every label. A 5% beer is 5% pure alcohol, so a 330 ml can holds 16.5 ml of it.

0.789

The density of alcohol, in grams per ml. It turns millilitres into grams: 16.5 ml × 0.789 ≈ 13 g.

Standard drink

Singapore's Health Promotion Board counts 10 g of alcohol as one standard drink. 13 g ÷ 10 = 1.3 drinks. The US uses 14 g and the UK uses 8 g, so the same can counts differently abroad.

05

How to approach it

Limits don't go up with experience. Tolerance changes how drunk you feel, not how much harm the alcohol does.

Beginner

1 to 2

standard drinks per session, at most

  • Start with beer, cider or a light wine
  • Skip shots and soju rounds at first
  • Eat first, and drink water between drinks
  • Drink with people you trust
  • Red face or racing heart: stop for the night

Regular

≤2 · ≤1

per day for men · for women (HPB guidance)

  • Pace at about 1 standard drink an hour
  • Keep 2 or more alcohol-free days a week
  • Know your drink's ABV, since pours vary
  • Don't mix spirits with energy drinks

Experienced

Same limits

tolerance doesn't make it safer

  • Feeling fine doesn't mean your body is
  • 4 to 5 or more drinks in 2 hours is a binge
  • Track your weekly total, not just nights out
  • Needing more to feel it is a sign to cut back

06

Stay in control

Only two things decide how drunk you get: how much alcohol you drink and how fast. Everything else just changes how quickly it reaches your blood.

Before and while you drink

Eat a proper meal firstFood, especially protein and fat, holds alcohol in your stomach longer. It can cut your peak level by around a third compared with an empty stomach.

Pick your number before you startDecide on 2 standard drinks, say, and count them. Once you're tipsy, your judgement about "one more" gets worse.

About 1 standard drink an hourThat's roughly how fast your liver clears it. Drink faster and it builds up.

A glass of water with every drinkIt slows you down and keeps your hands busy.

Don't let people top you upAt dinners and rounds you lose count. Finish your glass before it's refilled, or cover it.

Watch fizz and energy drinksCarbonation speeds up absorption. Energy drinks hide how drunk you are.

Drink less on an off dayBeing tired, dehydrated, ill or on medication makes alcohol hit harder.

The trick is more liquid for the same alcohol. A highball and a shot can hold the same 30 ml of spirit, but one takes 20 minutes to drink and the other takes 2 seconds. At toasts, raise your glass and take a small sip.

If you drank too much at one go

  1. Stop drinking nowYour blood alcohol keeps rising for 30 to 90 minutes after your last drink, so expect to feel worse before you feel better.
  2. Tell a friend and stay with themDon't walk home alone, swim, drive or take the stairs on your own.
  3. Sit down, sip water, eat something plainBread or crackers won't sober you up, but they slow down what's still in your stomach.
  4. Feeling sick? Sit up and lean forwardDon't make yourself vomit, because you can choke.
  5. Sleep on your side, never on your backIf it's a friend, check their breathing during the night.
  6. Skip painkillers that nightParacetamol with heavy drinking strains your liver, and ibuprofen irritates your stomach. Wait until the next day, after food.

Myths that don't work

Coffee sobers you upYou become a wide-awake drunk person who feels more in control than they are.
A cold shower helpsIt risks falls and chills, and your blood alcohol stays the same.
Throwing up gets it outMost of the alcohol is already absorbed, and you can choke.
A greasy meal afterwardsFood helps before you drink, not after it's in your blood.
Beer before liquor, never sickerThe order doesn't matter. The total amount does.
Hair of the dogA morning drink only delays the hangover.

Only time sobers you up.

Call 995 if someone has

Confusion, vomiting while drowsy, slow or irregular breathing, cold or bluish skin, a seizure, or can't be woken up. Lay them on their side, stay with them, and don't let them "sleep it off" alone.

Figures use typical ABVs and serving sizes. Real bottles vary by 1 to 2%, and cocktail and bar pours vary a lot more. Limits follow Singapore Health Promotion Board guidance. The WHO notes that no level of drinking is completely risk-free, so these are lower-risk limits, not safe ones.
