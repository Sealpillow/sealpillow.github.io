Know your pour

A can of beer, a glass of wine and a shot of whisky hold about the same amount of alcohol. A bottle of soju holds five times that. This guide shows how strong each drink really is, and how much is sensible for you.

01

How strength is worked out

Strength depends on two things: how big the drink is and how much of it is alcohol. Multiply them together and you get the amount of pure alcohol, which is what your body actually has to deal with.

Volume (ml)× ABV %× 0.789÷ 10 g= standard drinks

ABV

Alcohol by volume, printed on every label. A 5% beer is 5% pure alcohol, so a 330 ml can holds 16.5 ml of it.

0.789

The density of alcohol, in grams per ml. It turns millilitres into grams: 16.5 ml × 0.789 ≈ 13 g.

Standard drink

Singapore's Health Promotion Board counts 10 g of alcohol as one standard drink. 13 g ÷ 10 = 1.3 drinks. The US uses 14 g and the UK uses 8 g, so the same can counts differently abroad.

Wines, light to strong

A 5.5% Moscato has less than half the alcohol of a 15% Zinfandel. Bars often pour 175 ml or more, so switch the glass size to see what you're really drinking.

05

How to approach it

Limits don't go up with experience. Tolerance changes how drunk you feel, not how much harm the alcohol does.

Beginner

1 to 2

standard drinks per session, at most

  • Start with beer, cider or a light wine
  • Skip shots and soju rounds at first
  • Eat first, and drink water between drinks
  • Drink with people you trust
  • Red face or racing heart: stop for the night

Regular

≤2 · ≤1

per day for men · for women (HPB guidance)

  • Pace at about 1 standard drink an hour
  • Keep 2 or more alcohol-free days a week
  • Know your drink's ABV, since pours vary
  • Don't mix spirits with energy drinks

Experienced

Same limits

tolerance doesn't make it safer

  • Feeling fine doesn't mean your body is
  • 4 to 5 or more drinks in 2 hours is a binge
  • Track your weekly total, not just nights out
  • Needing more to feel it is a sign to cut back

If you go redAsian flush means your body is slow to clear acetaldehyde, a toxic by-product of alcohol. Regular drinking raises the risk of oesophageal cancer for people who flush.

Don't hide the flushAntihistamines mask the redness, not the harm.

Your liver clears about 1 standard drink an hour. Water, coffee and sleep don't speed it up. Never drive after drinking.

Call 995 if someone has

Confusion, vomiting while drowsy, slow or irregular breathing, cold or bluish skin, a seizure, or can't be woken up. Lay them on their side, stay with them, and don't let them "sleep it off" alone.

Figures use typical ABVs and serving sizes. Real bottles vary by 1 to 2%, and cocktail and bar pours vary a lot more. Limits follow Singapore Health Promotion Board guidance. The WHO notes that no level of drinking is completely risk-free, so these are lower-risk limits, not safe ones.

"Hours to clear" is a rough average and doesn't tell you when you're fit to drive.
